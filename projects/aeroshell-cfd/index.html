Aeroshell CFD (Catamaran Body)

Contributed to a RANS CFD model that corrected the solar car's predicted drag and lift.

Scope: Contributor, mechanical design, mesh refinement, ran simulations, post-processed results. Tool: ANSYS Fluent. Team: UBC Solar aeroshell subteam.

This project developed a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model of the UBC Solar catamaran aeroshell to predict aerodynamic drag and lift and to guide body geometry decisions. Working as part of the aeroshell team, I contributed to the mechanical design and mesh development, ran simulations, and post-processed the aerodynamic results. The goal was to build a model with enough near-wall fidelity to resolve flow separation and produce trustworthy drag and lift coefficients for design comparison.

Modelling Approach

The simulation used a steady, incompressible RANS solver in ANSYS Fluent with the k-omega SST turbulence model, chosen for its strength in resolving adverse pressure gradients and boundary layer separation on the curved catamaran surfaces. Flow conditions were set to a freestream velocity of 23 m/s, with drag and lift referenced to the catamaran frontal area of 0.985 square meters.

The Problem

The first iteration of the mesh did not resolve the near-wall region well enough to trust its separation behavior:

  • Around 2 million cells with only 3 inflation layers and a coarse surface resolution.
  • A y+ range of 1 to 500, leaving most of the near-wall region unresolved.
  • Poor flow separation prediction and low overall fidelity, which undermined confidence in the resulting force coefficients.

Mesh Refinement

To resolve the near-wall flow, the mesh was rebuilt with substantially higher resolution and tighter y+ control:

  • Cell count increased from roughly 2 million to 8.9 million.
  • Inflation layers increased from 3 to 24 or more, with a finer surface mesh.
  • y+ brought down from a 1 to 500 range to a controlled 0.5 to 5, resolving the near-wall region and greatly improving separation accuracy.

The Outcome

Resolving the near-wall region shifted the predicted coefficients between iterations, correcting the drag and lift estimates the team was designing against:

  • Predicted drag coefficient corrected from 0.176 to 0.128.
  • Predicted lift coefficient corrected from -1.0 to -0.55, where negative lift indicates intended downforce on the catamaran body.

The refined model gave the team more reliable pressure distributions and force predictions to compare body geometries against, rather than coefficients biased by an underresolved boundary layer.

On validation: this work is a contribution to a team model, not a solo effort, and the coefficients above have not yet been validated against physical or wind tunnel data. They represent an improvement in mesh quality and near-wall resolution between iterations, not a confirmed measurement.

Reflection and Next Steps

This project deepened my understanding of turbulence modelling, near-wall meshing, and the direct link between y+ control and separation prediction fidelity. It also reinforced how much a reported coefficient depends on mesh quality, and the importance of verifying near-wall resolution before trusting CFD output. Next steps would include a formal mesh independence study and validation of the predicted coefficients against physical or wind tunnel data.
