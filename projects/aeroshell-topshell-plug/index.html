UBC Solar Aeroshell Topshell Plug

CAD surfacing and plug development for a competition solar car body.

Scope: Lead surfacing designer, first 3 of 7 aeroshell development iterations. Tools: SolidWorks Surfacing. Team: UBC Solar aeroshell subteam.

I led the CAD surfacing and plug development process for the topshell of the UBC Solar Aeroshell, designing precision male molds used to manufacture composite body panels. The aeroshell fabrication workflow required three stages: surfacing a male mold with all feature indents, producing a fiberglass female mold from foam tooling, and completing a wet composite layup to create the final structure.

Initial Steps

I began by working with another team member and reverse engineering our previous vehicle into CAD to build a practice plug, which strengthened my surfacing workflow and deepened my understanding of aerodynamic geometry and airfoil continuity. Accuracy was critical, since misalignment in plugs would propagate into downstream components.

Final Product

I then produced a scaled prototype plug for process validation, incorporating 1.5 mm and 1 mm indents for fairings and lights. Offsetting complex surfaces at small radii created intersecting geometry, requiring iterative redesign and increased fillet radii to maintain manufacturability while improving airflow.

Building on these iterations, I designed the full topshell plug for our next-generation vehicle and prepared it for supplier quoting to evaluate machining cost and optimize geometry for manufacturability. This version integrated detailed features such as battery access panels, intake and exhaust openings, and auxiliary mounting points, demonstrating my ability to translate aerodynamic concepts into production-ready tooling models.
